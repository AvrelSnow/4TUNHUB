0. Assumptions (change one, and the route must be re-checked)

  1. The learner arrives at 4tunhub.com/academy with no account: an engineering student or junior technician in Cameroon or francophone Africa, on a phone or a shared laptop, time-poor, often on a slow line.
  2. Free and self-paced. No live coaching in this route. The taught offer is the cohort, which sits at the end, not inside.
  3. Real-only. A stage lists only what exists today. What does not exist is marked coming, with no link, and never counted in a time total.
  4. No account on the Academy site. Progress on the site lives on the learner's device. The Lab will have accounts; the Academy does not duplicate them.
  5. Language. The route is in English here. The site shows it in French too; the lessons and videos themselves are in English, and the page says so.

1. What the route is for

Two goals bring people here, and they are different:

Track A: Simulation Track B: SolidWorks and the CSWA
Who people who found the channel and want to run an LS-DYNA model correctly people who want the SOLIDWORKS CSWA certificate
End state builds, runs and checks a simple explicit or quasi-static model, and can say whether the result is believable models parts from a drawing and reports mass and centre of mass correctly, ready to practise the exam
Evidence a hand check of the units, then reading results against it the Lab's graded practice problems, answered correctly on a fresh seed

A shared foundation comes first for both: Engineering Thinking. Its stance ("physics first, software second") is the channel's own, and a learner who can turn a load into a force and check a stress is better at both tracks.

What the free route does not promise. It does not make anyone ready to book the CSWA. The taught course's bar is 190/240 on a full 180-minute timed mock, and no free timed mock exists. The free route's honest end state is ready to practise for the exam, and the cohort is where the mock lives.

3. Competencies (what the learner can do, observably)

Foundation (from the path's own "you will be able to", open cards only): - F1 Turn a hanging load into a force and a bending moment. - F2 Check a stress against an allowable stress with a safety factor. - F3 Choose a section and a plate thickness that can actually be bought. - F4 Check a deflection (sag) against a limit, so "strong" is not mistaken for "stiff".

Not yet testable here: reading a dimensioned drawing and its datums, and modelling the part and sending a STEP file. Those belong to card 5 and the boss, which need the Lab's checker and are closed.

Track A - A-1 Install LS-PrePost and get past the first crashes. - A-2 Build the ball-plate model: mesh, boundary conditions, contact, material and parts, solver set-up. - A-3 Read stress, displacement and energy, and judge whether the result is believable. - A-4 Edit a keyword deck by hand without breaking it, and know which file to open. - A-5 Check the unit system by hand before pressing run (P005). - A-6 Apply a static load to an explicit solver by ramping it (the bracket).

Track B - B-1 Get SOLIDWORKS running legally and free (the student offer). - B-2 Sketch and extrude/revolve a part from a drawing, with the right origin. - B-3 Report volume, mass and centre of mass for a part (P001, P002). - B-4 Drive a dimension with a global variable or an equation, and rebuild (P003). - B-5 Say what the exam asks and how it is structured.

Until the Lab opens, gates A and B are coming. The route says so rather than substituting a quiz that tests recall.

6. How the time was estimated, and what would change it

  • Content time is read from the library (lsdyna.ts) and the path data (engineering-thinking.json); two episodes have no length yet.
  • Working time = content time × 2 to 3 for follow-along videos (the learner pauses, builds the model and breaks it), and about ×1.2 for the path's lessons, which are already interactive. That multiplier is my assumption, not a measurement. The first ten learners are the measurement.
  • A schedule with dates is deliberately absent. The only honest unit is hours; dates would be invented, and the next cohort is not named in public. A rhythm such as "3 h a week" is a suggestion, not a promise.

8. Design review (what attacking the first draft found, and what changed)

  1. The path was counted wrong. The first reading was 13 lessons, 83 minutes. Card 5, the warm-up beam and the wall bracket are closed (they need the Lab's checker). Open: 11 lessons, 71 minutes. Corrected throughout.
  2. Track B had a hole at the bottom. The free route had nothing structured to teach SOLIDWORKS basics: MySolidWorks, Hawk Ridge and the French playlists were removed from the list on purpose. The taught course makes basics a mandatory Week 0 plus an entry diagnostic for this reason. Repaired 2026-11-24 (Avrel chose option a): B2 now links SOLIDWORKS' own Getting Started tutorials, read that day (open, no account; four lessons, 2 h 30). They also ship in the software (swtutorial.chm is in the 2023 install on this machine). Still open: they are English only; the page is the 2024 edition (the 2026 address does not exist); they teach the interface, parts, assemblies and drawings but not mass properties or the exam, which is what B3 and the practice problems are for; and there is no entry check like the cohort's diagnostic, so a learner can enter B3 without having done B2.
  3. The dashboard cannot be what was imagined. Only the path's progress can be tracked by the site; videos and Lab results cannot. §7 now says so.
  4. Not every practice problem belongs on the CSWA path. P005 is a simulation problem; P004 and P006 are drawing and tolerance arithmetic that the CSWA does not test heavily. They are placed on Track A and the Tolerances branch, not forced into Track B.
  5. Video time is not learning time. Using content time as the schedule would have undersold the route by half or more. Working time is now shown, with its assumption.
  6. Track A has no "apply" stage. After the bracket, a learner has watched and followed but never built a model on their own. No free problem of that kind exists. It is shown as missing (A4), not hidden.
  7. Over-claiming. "Ready for the CSWA" would be false: the readiness bar needs a timed mock. The end state was rewritten to "ready to practise".
  8. A dependency was guessed. Whether Engineering Thinking's first two lessons ("kilogram", "units") cover mass and units as CSWA answers need them has not been read; they are suggested for Track B, to be confirmed.
