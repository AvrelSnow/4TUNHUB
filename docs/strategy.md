Assets that exist today:

| The REM (LinkedIn newsletter) | 3,200+ readers | Reach among energy/engineering professionals. The largest audience he has. |

| Students taught (IUC, ENSET, University of Douala, Lycée de Fongo-Tongo) | 300+ | Proof of teaching, and a network of future buyers. |
| @4TUNHUB YouTube (LS-DYNA, English) | ~20 subscribers | A niche with few good tutorials. Worth building. Not an audience yet. |

| Six documented projects | drawings, FEA, test results | Teaching material nobody else has. |

**The bottleneck is audience and trust, not the number of products.** A
catalogue of ten products with nobody to sell to earns nothing. One course
sold to people who already trust him earns money in month one.

"The go-to platform in Africa" is the ten-year destination. The next twelve
months decide whether it gets there, and they are about one thing: **the
first paid product that people actually buy.**

## 2. Positioning

The market has a hole in it:

- **India proved the model.** Skill-Lync sells job-oriented CAE programmes
  (HyperMesh, ANSA, LS-DYNA, ANSYS) built around portfolio projects, at
  INR 25,000–35,000 a month. Engineers pay for *employability*, not for
  videos.
- **Anglophone Africa has CAD schools** (CAD4ALL in South Africa, accredited,
  strong placement numbers), but they are drafting-focused and far away.
- **Francophone Central and West Africa has nothing credible** for practical
  mechanical engineering: SolidWorks certification, simulation, GD&T and
  drawing standards, taught from machines built locally. French-language
  SolidWorks/CSWA prep exists, but it is written for France, priced in euros
  and paid by card.

**Proposed position:** *The practical mechanical-engineering school for
francophone Africa, taught by a certified engineer from real machines built
here.*

Two markets, run deliberately in parallel:

1. **Local, in French:** Cameroon first, then CEMAC, then francophone Africa.
   SolidWorks, certification prep, GD&T, final-year projects. Priced in FCFA,
   paid by Mobile Money.
2. **Global, in English:** LS-DYNA and explicit dynamics. The YouTube channel
   is the top of this funnel. The niche is small but global and underserved,
   and it pays in dollars.

The rest of the site (Services, Research, Projects) is what makes the
Academy credible. Services also earns money while the Academy grows.

## 3. What to sell: the product ladder

Ranked by how soon each can earn and how well it fits. **Build them in
this order. Do not start the next rung until the one before has sold.**

### Rung 1: first sale within 90 days

> **Changed 2026-09-18:** Fortune decided the first cohort is **free**, and
> it launches next week. See `docs/launch-plan.md`. The paid cohort below
> becomes Cohort 1, sold on Cohort 0's results.

**A. CSWA preparation cohort, in French.** *(first product)*
- Why first: he holds the CSWP and the CSWE, so he is overqualified to teach
  it. The outcome is binary and valuable: the student passes or fails a
  globally recognised certificate. The buyers are already in the
  SolidWorks group and the WhatsApp community. No French-language, FCFA,
  Mobile Money offer exists.
- Format: live 4–6 week cohort (sessions on Meet or Zoom, homework in
  WhatsApp), recorded. The recording becomes the self-paced version later.
- Price: *(verify with a pre-sale)* test 35,000–60,000 FCFA for the cohort.
  An early-bird price for the first 10 seats.
- Rule: **pre-sell before building.** Announce it, take payment, run it only
  if 10 people pay. If fewer do, ask them why. That answer is worth more
  than the course.

**B. Services.** Open to any client since he left CAMRAIL. With the
Fongo-Tongo salary as his only fixed income, one client job is the fastest
route to cash while the Academy grows.

### Rung 2: months 3–6

**C. GD&T and technical drawing, in French** (his own idea, and a good one).
- Hook: the braking project. The tolerance that assumed workshop
  capabilities nobody had, and the rework it cost. Open the course with that
  story; it is the course's reason to exist.
- Teach ISO GPS alongside ASME Y14.5, because francophone industry works
  in ISO. Name it clearly as a certificate of completion, *not* the ASME
  GDTP certification. Never let it be confused with that.
- Benchmark: fundamentals courses sell for about $295–$800 in the US. In
  FCFA the price has to be local, but the value is real.

**D. Downloads that come out of the courses** (cheap to make, low price,
the entry point for people who aren't ready to buy a course):
- Design calculators: shaft, bearing, belt and pulley, and weld sizing
  sheets. He has already done these calculations in the shredder, pedal
  charger and beans-machine projects.
- Drawing templates: ISO title blocks, sheet formats, a tolerance table.
- Complete project dossiers (drawings, bill of materials, calculation
  notes) for one machine each, sold as *reference study material*.

### Rung 3: months 6–12

**E. Final-year project support ("projet de fin d'études") for BTS/HND and
licence students.** He teaches BTS at IUC and sees this need every year.
Offered as coaching and method (how to size, draw and validate a machine),
**never** as writing the project for them. The line between teaching and
ghost-writing has to be explicit, or it will damage the brand he is
building with institutions.

**F. LS-DYNA foundations, in English, priced in USD.** Only once the
YouTube channel shows demand (comments asking for more, a few hundred
subscribers). The channel is where that demand shows up first.

**G. Training for institutions and companies.** License the curriculum to
technical schools and give in-house SolidWorks/GD&T training to industrial
companies. The highest ticket, but it needs a track record (rungs 1–2)
first. He has left CAMRAIL, so rail clients are open; only a
confidentiality clause in his old contract, if there was one, still limits
what he shows publicly.

### Deliberately NOT now

- **Software products** (educational assistant, digital twin, AI tools).
  Building software is expensive and slow, and it needs its own buyers. It
  comes after education earns money. The Products page stays hidden until
  something ships.
- **Printed books.** Low margin, heavy logistics. An e-book carved out of
  the GD&T course later is fine, as a cheap entry product.
- **Building our own course platform (LMS).** Use existing tools until
  revenue justifies building one.

## 4. The engine: how the parts feed each other

```
YouTube / REM / LinkedIn / WhatsApp   (free: attention and trust)
            │
            ▼
   Projects & Research on the site     (proof: "he actually builds this")
            │
            ▼
   Email list + WhatsApp community     (owned audience: the real asset)
            │
            ▼
   Academy (courses, downloads)  ◀──▶  Services (client work)
            │                               │
            └──── new projects to teach ◀───┘
```

Every project becomes a course module. Every course points to a project as
proof. Every client job becomes a new case study. That loop is the business.

## 5. What each section of the site has to do

**Home.** One promise and two doors: *learn* (student or young engineer →
Academy) and *hire* (company → Services). Proof in between. Nothing else.

**Academy** (the commercial heart). For every course:
- outcome in one line ("pass the CSWA"), who it's for, and prerequisites;
- a full syllabus, and the project you build in it;
- instructor credentials (CSWP/CSWE/DIPET), shown next to the price;
- price in FCFA; Mobile Money (MTN, Orange) and card;
- a free first lesson (a YouTube video);
- until it opens, a waitlist that collects **email and WhatsApp number**.

**Projects.** Each case study ends with "learn how this was done" →
the matching course module, and "need something like this?" → Services.

**Services.** Add a clear "how to start" section (brief → quote → timeline)
and, as soon as it exists, one client testimonial.

**Research.** The REM archive, organised by topic, plus a subscribe
button. This is where the energy audience lands.

**Community.** It becomes real by showing the work of its members:
- a weekly CAD challenge in the WhatsApp group ("Friday challenge"), with
  the best submissions shown on the site;
- a monthly live session (Q&A, a project walkthrough);
- clear rules and separate channels by level (student / professional).
A community page with members' work on it is proof that no competitor can
fake.

**About / Founder.** Stays as it is. It is the credibility layer.

## 6. The infrastructure that has to exist before the first sale

1. **An email list.** The one audience no platform can take away. Email is
   reported as the largest single source of sales for independent sellers
   of digital products. A signup form on every Academy page, starting now.
2. **Mobile Money payments.** In Cameroon, MTN MoMo and Orange Money are
   how people pay online. Card-only checkout loses most local buyers.
   Aggregators that cover Cameroon: CinetPay, NotchPay, Moneroo. The choice
   depends on fees and paperwork *(verify, and check what registration a
   business needs)*.
3. **Course hosting.** Start on an existing platform that accepts Mobile
   Money, or deliver the first cohort live with recordings shared
   privately. The site links to wherever the course lives.
4. **A domain and a real email address**, needed anyway for launch.
