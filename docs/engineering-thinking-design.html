Proposal for review · 25 November 2026 · nothing on the site has changed

Engineering Thinking is a drawing office. You build one part by learning.

The loud thing on screen is the part: drawn large, gaining pieces as you clear cards, then taking its load. Rewards are things an engineer knows: stamps, a title block, a licence. White pages, black and amber, as on 4tunhub.com.

Built with CSS and inline drawings, with no animation library to start. Brilliant uses Rive for its animations; that is worth revisiting only if drawings by hand stop being enough. Every effect switches off for people who ask for reduced motion.

Left out on purpose

Leaderboards and live learner counts (need a server). XP and gems (your Lab design dropped XP; it can be added if you want it). Confetti. Guilt messages about a broken streak. Locks on lessons: the order is recommended, not forced.

4 · What the research says

  • A meta-analysis of gamified learning (Sailer and Homner) found small positive effects: learning g = 0.49, motivation 0.36, behaviour 0.25. The learning result held up better in rigorous studies than the other two. Game story mattered for behaviour.
  • Duolingo moved to one guided path because learners were unsure they were using the app the right way. Some users disliked losing choice.
  • A study of 859 streak designs argues streaks count days rather than learning, and that fear of breaking one can backfire.
